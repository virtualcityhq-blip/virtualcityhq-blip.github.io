For a pocket-fit check, use the manufacturer’s closed notebook dimensions: 3 × 5.25 × 0.375 inches for No. 135, and 4 × 6.25 × 0.375 inches for No. 846. Measure the opening as well as the inside of your pocket. Leave room to grasp the notebook; a rectangle that just fits is not a comfortable fit test.

Before buying, draw a 3 × 5 rectangle and a 4 × 6 rectangle on scrap paper. Try the five-line record below in each at your normal handwriting size. If the smaller one feels cramped, the larger sheet has a purpose. If both work, let the carrying space decide.

Choose a writing tool that works.

Rite in the Rain says a standard pencil works on its paper wet or dry. That makes a pencil you already own a sensible starting point. The company also supports its all-weather pens for wet writing; a special pen is optional.

Its guidance distinguishes ordinary ballpoints, which work on dry paper, from water-based inks. The manufacturer advises against gel pens, fountain pens, and highlighters on this paper. Check your writing tool before taking the notebook outside. Manufacturer’s writing guidance ↗

A five-line record.

Give each outing a page. You do not need to reach a summit to have a day worth recording.

PLACE: peak, trail, or crag; the route taken.
PEOPLE: names, not just a group photo.
DAY: date, start and finish; what you observed.
DECISION: where you continued, changed plans, or turned.
DETAIL: one thing you want to remember.

Write when you are stopped somewhere appropriate. These are personal notes, not a navigation system, route guide, or substitute for current conditions and your own judgment.

What those five lines can hold.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE. NOT A REPORTED TRIP OR ROUTE ADVICE.

PLACE
The trail above the lake; returned by the same path.
PEOPLE
Alex and Sam.
DAY
Saturday; left the trailhead at 7:10, back at 11:40.
DECISION
Turned at the ridge when the group chose a shorter day.
DETAIL
Sam spotted a small patch of ice still holding in the shade.

At home, expand one line while it is fresh: what led to the shorter day, what worked, or what you would pack differently. Keep observations separate from guesses. A blank line is better than an invented detail.
